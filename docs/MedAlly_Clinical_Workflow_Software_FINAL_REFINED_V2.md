# Clinical Workflow Software for Physicians Across the Patient Encounter

Clinical workflow software helps coordinate the tasks, information, reviews, and handoffs that make up clinical work.

The category is broader than documentation alone. Depending on the product and care setting, clinical workflow software can support areas such as encounter documentation, patient flow, task coordination, clinical review, follow-up, coding, operational handoffs, and connections between systems.

## What Is Clinical Workflow Software?

Clinical workflow software is software that helps organize how clinical tasks and information move between people, systems, and stages of care.

The exact scope varies significantly by product.

For example, one platform may focus on patient flow and staff coordination. Another may focus on scheduling, prior authorization, or operational task routing. Another may center on encounter documentation, physician review, follow-up, coding, or downstream handoff.

That distinction matters when comparing products: **"clinical workflow software" describes a category, not one standardized feature set.**

Healthcare workflow software can be an even broader term because it may include administrative, financial, access, and operational workflows in addition to clinician-facing clinical work.

## What Problems Can Clinical Workflow Software Address?

Different workflow problems require different software capabilities. Before comparing vendors, first identify which problem the practice is trying to solve.

| Workflow problem | Capability to evaluate |
|---|---|
| Documentation takes too many separate steps | Encounter capture, note preparation, editing, and clinician review |
| Clinicians cannot easily see what requires review | Review queues, status visibility, clear clinician-validation steps |
| Follow-up work becomes disconnected from the encounter | Follow-up capture, ownership, status, and next-step workflow |
| Coding information is prepared separately from the clinical work | Coding context tied to the reviewed encounter |
| Work has to move between multiple systems | Integration, export, handoff, or transfer methods |
| Staff lose visibility into where work or patients are in a process | Task/status coordination, patient-flow visibility, notifications, or routing |
| Administrative work slows clinical operations | Scheduling, intake, authorization, referral, or revenue-cycle workflow support, where relevant |

A product does not need to address every row. The goal is to match the software to the workflow problem that actually exists.

## Clinical Workflow Software vs. AI Medical Scribe vs. Clinical Documentation AI

These categories overlap, but they are not interchangeable.

| Category | Primary focus | Typical scope |
|---|---|---|
| **AI medical scribe** | Capturing the encounter and helping prepare a note draft | One part of the documentation workflow |
| **AI clinical documentation** | Structuring, reviewing, and editing clinical documentation | Documentation-focused workflow |
| **Clinical workflow software** | Coordinating multiple clinical tasks, reviews, handoffs, or process stages | Broader than documentation alone |
| **Healthcare workflow software** | Clinical plus potentially administrative and operational workflows | Often the broadest term |

## What Should a Practice Evaluate Before Choosing Clinical Workflow Software?

A useful evaluation goes beyond a feature checklist. Ask the vendor to show how the product behaves in the workflow your clinicians actually use.

| Evaluation area | What to ask the vendor to show |
|---|---|
| **Documentation** | A realistic sample draft and the physician editing/review process |
| **Clinician review** | Which outputs require clinician validation and how pending work is identified |
| **Follow-up** | How a follow-up action is created, assigned, recorded, or tracked, where supported |
| **Coding** | How coding information is connected to the encounter and what must be reviewed |
| **Handoff** | The actual method used to move approved work to the next system or workflow step |
| **Integration** | Which systems are supported, what setup is required, and what varies by deployment |
| **Implementation** | Required configuration, training, workflow changes, and responsibilities |
| **Governance** | Which actions are automated, which require approval, and how user roles are handled |
| **Measurement** | Which workflow outcomes the practice can monitor after implementation |

Do not accept broad terms such as "automated workflow" without seeing where automation starts, where clinician review occurs, and what happens after approval.

## How Can a Practice Test Workflow Fit Before Implementation?

A product demonstration is more useful when it follows a real practice scenario rather than a generic feature tour.

A practical evaluation can use a fictional or de-identified encounter and ask the vendor to demonstrate:

1. what information enters the workflow;
2. what the software prepares or changes;
3. what the clinician must review;
4. what happens when the clinician edits or rejects an output;
5. how follow-up or coding work is handled;
6. how approved work reaches the next step;
7. what requires configuration or integration before go-live.

This approach helps reveal whether a product fits the existing workflow or requires the practice to redesign it around the software.

## What Should Be Confirmed Before a Clinical Workflow Software Rollout?

Before implementation, confirm the practical details that determine whether a workflow will function in production:

### Workflow boundaries

Document which parts of the current process the software will support and which remain outside the product.

### Physician responsibilities

Define what clinicians must review, edit, validate, or approve before work moves forward.

### Integration and handoff

Confirm the actual systems involved, the transfer method, implementation requirements, and any deployment-specific limitations.

### Follow-up ownership

Clarify who is responsible for follow-up actions and where their status is tracked.

### Coding and billing review

Establish who validates coding and billing-related information before use.

### Training and change management

Identify what clinicians and staff need to learn and whether the existing workflow must change.

A workflow product should be evaluated in the context of the practice's real process, not just its feature list.

## How Is Clinical Workflow Software Different From an EHR?

An EHR is the system used to maintain the patient record and support a wide range of clinical and administrative functions.

Clinical workflow software focuses on how particular tasks, reviews, information, and handoffs move through a process. Depending on the product, it may work alongside the EHR rather than replace it.

For MedAlly, the relevant question is how physician-approved work moves into the next practice or EHR workflow step. That method varies by deployment and should be confirmed during evaluation.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Clinical Workflow Software

### What should a practice evaluate before choosing clinical workflow software?

Start with the workflow problem, then ask vendors to demonstrate the actual process: what enters the system, what the software prepares, what clinicians must review, how exceptions are handled, and how approved work reaches the next step.

### How is encounter documentation different from workflow coordination?

Encounter documentation focuses on capturing and structuring the clinical note. Workflow coordination is broader and can include review responsibilities, follow-up, task or status management, coding, handoff, integration, and other steps surrounding the encounter.

### Is healthcare workflow software the same as clinical workflow software?

Not always. The terms can overlap, but healthcare workflow software may include broader administrative and operational processes in addition to clinician-facing clinical workflows.

### What should a practice confirm about EHR handoff?

Confirm the exact transfer method, which systems are supported, what requires clinician approval, what setup is needed, and which behaviors vary by deployment. Do not assume that "EHR integration" means automatic chart commitment.

### How can a practice test whether software fits its workflow?

Use a realistic fictional or de-identified scenario and ask the vendor to demonstrate the complete path from input through review, edits, exceptions, approval, and downstream handoff.

### Where does MedAlly fit within clinical workflow software?

MedAlly focuses on the physician encounter workflow: encounter listening, SOAP-style documentation, reviewable clinical information, treatment/follow-up support, ICD-10/CPT and billing-related information, physician review, and the next workflow step after approval.

### Does MedAlly replace physician review?

No. MedAlly prepares and organizes information for review. Clinical judgment and final clinical decisions remain with the physician.

### Can MedAlly support an EHR-connected workflow?

MedAlly can support practice / EHR workflow handoff after physician review and approval. The exact integration and handoff method vary by deployment.
