# How MedAlly Works Across the Patient Encounter

MedAlly listens during the patient encounter and helps turn the visit into structured, reviewable work. It prepares SOAP-style clinical documentation, organizes clinical and lab information for review, prepares ICD-10/CPT and billing information, and supports follow-up and workflow handoff after physician review.

Forever Free includes **10 encounters per month**.

## How Does MedAlly Work?

MedAlly uses the patient encounter as the starting point for the work that follows. Instead of rebuilding the visit across separate tools, physicians can move from the encounter to documentation, clinical review, coding and billing information, follow-up, and the next workflow step in one connected process.

**Encounter → AI-prepared work → physician review → approved next step**

## The MedAlly Workflow in Six Steps

### 1. MedAlly listens during the patient encounter

During the visit, MedAlly listens to the patient-physician conversation and uses the encounter information to prepare the work that follows.

That encounter becomes the basis for the clinical note and other reviewable outputs, so the physician does not have to reconstruct the visit from a blank page afterward.

For the encounter-capture and note-drafting workflow, see **[MedAlly AI Medical Scribe](/ai-medical-scribe)**.

### 2. MedAlly prepares SOAP-style clinical documentation

MedAlly turns the encounter into **SOAP-style clinical documentation** prepared for physician review.

The physician receives a structured draft that can be reviewed and edited before it becomes part of the final clinical record.

For the documentation-specific workflow, see **[AI Clinical Documentation](/clinical-documentation-ai)**.

### 3. MedAlly organizes clinical information for review

The visit can create clinical work beyond the note itself. MedAlly can organize:

- decision-support and differential-review information;
- lab-related information;
- treatment-planning information; and
- follow-up information connected to the encounter.

These outputs are presented to support clinician review and judgment, not to replace them.

### 4. MedAlly prepares coding and billing information

MedAlly prepares **ICD-10/CPT coding information** and related billing information around the same encounter.

Keeping this information connected to the visit gives the physician a clearer review path between the clinical documentation and the administrative work that follows.

Coding and billing information should be reviewed and validated before use.

### 5. The physician reviews and edits the prepared work

The physician reviews the SOAP-style note and the other information MedAlly has prepared, makes changes where needed, and validates the information that will be used.

This review can include:

- editing the clinical note;
- checking decision-support or differential information;
- reviewing lab-related information;
- validating ICD-10/CPT and billing information; and
- reviewing treatment-planning or follow-up information.

Clinical decisions and the final clinical record remain the physician's responsibility.

### 6. Approved work moves to the next workflow step

After physician review and approval, MedAlly can hand approved work back into the practice or EHR workflow.

The exact handoff method can vary by deployment.

## What MedAlly Produces From the Encounter

| Output | What MedAlly prepares | Physician action |
|---|---|---|
| Clinical note | SOAP-style clinical documentation | Review and edit the note |
| Decision-support information | Differential-review and supporting clinical information | Interpret and decide clinically |
| Lab information | Lab-related information connected to the visit | Review alongside the patient's clinical picture |
| Treatment and follow-up information | Treatment-planning and follow-up information | Decide the appropriate next action |
| Coding and billing information | ICD-10/CPT and related billing information | Review and validate before use |
| Workflow handoff | Approved work prepared for the next practice or EHR step | Approve what moves forward |

## Frequently Asked Questions About How MedAlly Works

### What information does MedAlly use to start the workflow?

MedAlly starts with the patient encounter. It listens to the patient-physician conversation and uses information from that encounter to prepare the work that follows.

### What does MedAlly prepare from the encounter?

MedAlly can prepare SOAP-style clinical documentation, decision-support and differential-review information, lab-related information, treatment-planning and follow-up information, ICD-10/CPT coding information, and related billing information for physician review.

### Can physicians edit the documentation MedAlly prepares?

Yes. Physicians can review and edit the prepared clinical documentation before it is used as part of the final clinical record.

### Does MedAlly provide ICD-10 and CPT coding information?

Yes. MedAlly prepares ICD-10/CPT coding information around the encounter for physician review. Coding information should be reviewed and validated before use.

### Does MedAlly provide decision-support and lab information?

Yes. MedAlly can organize decision-support, differential-review, and lab-related information around the encounter for physician review.

### Does MedAlly support treatment planning and follow-up?

Yes. MedAlly supports treatment-planning information and follow-up workflow connected to the encounter. The physician remains responsible for clinical decisions and next actions.

### How does approved work move into the existing workflow?

After physician review and approval, MedAlly can hand approved work back into the practice or EHR workflow. The exact handoff method can vary by deployment.

### Does MedAlly make clinical decisions for the physician?

No. MedAlly prepares and organizes information for review. Clinical judgment and final clinical decisions remain with the physician.

### Is MedAlly only an AI medical scribe?

No. The AI medical scribe is one part of a broader workflow that also includes structured documentation, clinical information for review, coding and billing information, treatment and follow-up work, and workflow handoff.
