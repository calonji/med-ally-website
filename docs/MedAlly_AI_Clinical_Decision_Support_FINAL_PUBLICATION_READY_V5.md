## What Is AI Clinical Decision Support?

Clinical decision support is designed to provide timely information that helps inform decisions about patient care. The U.S. Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality describes CDS as providing knowledge and person-specific information at appropriate times to support health and healthcare decisions. See the **[AHRQ Clinical Decision Support overview](https://www.ahrq.gov/topics/clinical-decision-support-cds.html)**.

**AI clinical decision support** applies artificial intelligence to help organize, surface, or prepare information that may be relevant to clinical review.

The exact function varies by product. Some clinical decision support systems focus on alerts, reminders, order sets, or narrow rule-based interventions. Others use AI to help synthesize larger amounts of clinical information.

For MedAlly, AI clinical decision support is **review-assisted**: the system prepares supporting information for the physician, while interpretation, clinical judgment, and final decisions remain with the clinician.

## How AI Fits Within Clinical Decision Support

Clinical decision support is a broad category, and **AI is one approach that can be used within it**. A single CDS product may use AI, rule-based logic, reference content, alerts, or a combination of methods. The important distinction is not whether a system is "AI" or "traditional," but what functions it actually provides and how clinicians are expected to use the output.

| Scope | Typical focus | What to verify |
|---|---|---|
| **Clinical decision support broadly** | Can include alerts, reminders, order sets, reference information, diagnostic support, and other patient-specific interventions | Which CDS functions are actually included and how they fit the clinical workflow |
| **AI-enabled clinical decision support** | Uses AI for one or more decision-support functions, such as organizing, synthesizing, or surfacing information relevant to review | What AI contributes, what other methods are used, and what requires clinician review |
| **MedAlly's verified decision-support scope** | Encounter-linked differential-review information, lab-related context, treatment-planning information, and follow-up context | What the physician reviews, interprets, decides, and approves before work moves forward |

MedAlly should not be assumed to provide every capability associated with the broader clinical decision support system category. Its verified role is to prepare and organize reviewable information around the encounter.

## What Should Physicians Evaluate in AI Clinical Decision Support?

The phrase "AI-powered" does not explain how a clinical decision-support product actually works.

When evaluating a system, physicians and clinical teams should ask:

| Evaluation area | What to clarify |
|---|---|
| **Clinical inputs** | What patient, encounter, documentation, and lab information the system uses |
| **Output** | What decision-support information the system prepares or surfaces |
| **Differential support** | How differential-review information is presented and what the clinician is expected to validate |
| **Lab context** | How lab-related information is incorporated into review |
| **Treatment-planning support** | What information is presented and where clinician decision-making remains required |
| **Follow-up** | How next-step information stays connected to the encounter |
| **Evidence and currency** | What sources inform the output, whether clinicians can inspect them, and how their relevance and currency are maintained |
| **Validation and limitations** | What evaluation supports the intended use, what limitations are documented, and where information may be incomplete or unreliable |
| **Clinician control** | What requires physician review, interpretation, approval, or action |
| **Workflow connection** | How decision-support information relates to documentation and downstream workflow |
| **Integration / handoff** | What happens after review and how the next practice or EHR step works |
| **Scope boundaries** | Which CDS functions the product does and does not provide |

MedAlly can present structured evidence-level summaries with evidence criteria, examples, limitations, and clinical applicability. Its Clinical Guidelines view can also display named guideline organizations, guideline/version labels and years, key recommendations, evidence-level descriptions, applicability, and limitations. Teams that require direct source-document links, article-level citations, or publication-level traceability should confirm how those references are exposed in their workflow or deployment.

The most important distinction is whether AI output is presented as information to review or as a substitute for clinical judgment. With MedAlly, clinical judgment remains with the physician.
